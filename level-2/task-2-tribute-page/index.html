Marie Curie

Two Nobel Prizes, one lifetime of curiosity.

Her Story

Marie Curie was born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw in 1867, at a time when Poland was under Russian rule and women were not allowed to study at university there. She worked as a governess to help pay for her sister's medical studies, saving what she could for her own education. In 1891 she moved to Paris and enrolled at the Sorbonne, where she often lived on very little money and went on to earn degrees in physics and mathematics.

In Paris she met the physicist Pierre Curie, and they married in 1895. Curious about the strange rays given off by uranium, she began to study them and gave the phenomenon the name "radioactivity". Working in a cramped, poorly equipped shed, she and Pierre discovered two new elements in 1898, polonium, which she named after her homeland, and radium. Separating a tiny amount of pure radium meant years of hard, repetitive labour.

In 1903 she shared the Nobel Prize in Physics, becoming the first woman to win a Nobel Prize. After Pierre's sudden death in 1906 she carried on their work and became the first woman to teach as a professor at the Sorbonne. In 1911 she won a second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry, making her the first person to win in two different sciences. During the First World War she helped build mobile X-ray units so that surgeons could find bullets and shrapnel in wounded soldiers, and she drove to the front herself.

Marie Curie died in 1934 from an illness linked to her long exposure to radiation, and her notebooks are still radioactive today. Her research helped open the way to modern physics and to radiation treatment for cancer, and her daughter Irène later won a Nobel Prize too. What I admire most is her patience: she kept going through poverty, loss and unfair barriers, and she never stopped asking questions.

Key Achievements

  1. 1867

    Born in Warsaw, Poland.

  2. 1891

    Moves to Paris to study at the Sorbonne.

  3. 1898

    Discovers polonium and radium with Pierre Curie.

  4. 1903

    Wins the Nobel Prize in Physics, the first woman to receive a Nobel.

  5. 1911

    Wins the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, her second.

  6. 1914

    Develops mobile X-ray units to help wounded soldiers in the First World War.

  7. 1934

    Dies in France, leaving a lasting legacy in science and medicine.
